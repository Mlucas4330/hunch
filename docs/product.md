# Product

## Overview

**The reader owns the landing page.** They paste their own URL, get a score out of 100 measured on the
page, and unlock the ranked fixes, each with a rationale and, where the change is a line of text, the
new copy already written.

**They built it with an AI tool, and that is the buyer this is now written for.** A Brazilian
creator, developer or designer who shipped something with Lovable, v0, Bolt or Cursor and cannot tell
whether what came out is any good. It replaces the earlier buyer, "the founder whose landing page is
not converting", and the swap matters because **the two do not share a problem.**

The founder has traffic and no signups: the pain is a loss they can already see. This reader has no
traffic at all -- they deployed last week. Their pain is **not knowing**, which is a different
sentence and a different first screen. Copy written for the first one talks past the second, which is
why the repositioning is copy before it is anything else.

**What makes them a good fit is not sympathy, it is the shape of what they have.** They own exactly
one artefact: a deployed URL. No analytics history, no traffic to A/B test, often no repo they would
let anybody near. A product that needs the URL and nothing else is the only kind of audit they can
buy, and it is what this already was.

**The buyer is the page owner, not a consultant selling audits to someone else.** There is no
white-label, no plan, no print report and no sale that needs a call: a brand system across four
surfaces and a deal closed by a person are both bets on a buyer nobody has spoken to. The score is a
smaller promise the product can already keep.

**"Anti AI slop" is the position, and it is three claims that must not be confused.** They have
different costs and different truth conditions:

1. **The analysis is not slop.** A chat writes plausible advice about a page it never opened; this
   opens the page in a real browser and counts. This is copy, and it is checkable in one click.
2. **The reader's page carries marks of sameness.** Counted in the DOM by the `sameness` group in
   `lib/readout.ts`: gradients, how many typefaces render, icons from a stock set, rows of three
   cards, emoji in headings, generic button labels, leftover placeholder text, a logo strip that
   links nowhere, a declared builder, a stock hero image.
3. **The fixes address those marks**, under `samenessRules` in `lib/ai/prompt.ts`, which is where
   (2) stops being a count and starts being advice. **None of them is graded**: a gradient is a
   choice, the reader is shown the counts without a verdict, and a fix may argue the mechanism on
   this page and never call the mark a defect.

**None of the three may say a page was generated by AI.** Nobody measured how a page was made, and a
hand-written page uses a gradient and a lucide icon too -- this repo's own `UnlockWall` uses both.
Naming the tools as context is fine and is what makes a reader recognise themselves; asserting that
pages from those tools convert worse is a number nobody has. See [ads.md](ads.md).

## One stage, and that is the whole product

Paste a URL, get an analysis. It needs **the URL and nothing else**: no access to the site, no
cooperation from a developer, nothing installed anywhere. See [report.md](report.md).

**There is no second stage.** A live A/B test measuring a chosen variant against the control on
real traffic is not part of this product, and the reasons are worth keeping:

- **It cannot be validated end to end from here.** It needs a snippet installed on a real client
  site, with a real CSP and a real tag manager.
- **Most readers could not use it.** Required sample scales with `(1-p)/p`: on a form-fill goal
  converting at 2%, detecting a 25% lift needs about 27,600 impressions. A landing page doing a
  thousand visitors a month never gets there, so the window closes with no verdict.
- **It asks for the hardest thing in the sale**: access to someone else's site plus a developer
  marking an element.

The consequence to keep in mind: **nothing in this product makes a causal claim.** The readout says
what was counted, and no surface says what a change will produce, see
[invariants.md](invariants.md#a-delta-is-arithmetic-between-two-measurements-never-a-result-attributed-to-a-change).

## What it does

**Analyze**

- Paste a landing page URL and generate ranked wording fixes, each with the replacement copy written
- Get a ranked flow playbook alongside them: structural fixes with implementation steps (offer login
  with Google, cut the signup form, add a Q&A block, repeat the CTA after pricing), grounded in a
  measured structural readout of the page rather than in a guess at what it contains
- Get a discoverability audit alongside them: what the page declares about itself (title, description,
  canonical, structured data) and what its robots.txt allows
- Get a measured readout of the page itself, counted, never generated (see [readout.md](readout.md))
- Optionally add a business brief so variants come back as finished, ready-to-ship copy

**Decide**

- Browse the lines that were judged not to be doing their job, each with a verdict on the line as it
  stands and one AI-recommended replacement. A page whose lines mostly work comes back with a short
  list rather than a padded one
- Ask for two alternate options per idea, written on demand from the analysis screen
- See the recommended copy rendered onto a screenshot of the real page before handing it over

**Read**

- Three posts at `/blog`, written for the person who arrived from an ad: what SEO is, what copy is,
  and what changes now that people ask an assistant instead of searching. Each one ends on the same
  button as the landing page. See [analysis-ui.md](analysis-ui.md#the-blog).

**Share**

- Share the analysis link (`/r/<embedKey>`), read with no session and authorized by the opaque key
  alone. It is the same page the owner reads, minus the controls that spend something. See
  [report.md](report.md).

**Account**

- Sign up, log in, log out via Google, or GitHub when it is configured
- Switch language between English and Brazilian Portuguese
- Track past pages as a grid on the dashboard, one card per analyzed landing page

## How a customer pays

**Credits, bought outright.** One credit unlocks one full analysis; the score is always free. Two
packs on the home page, R$97 for one analysis, R$247 for three, and a webhook grants once the
payment is confirmed. A third card sits beside them describing the free half, which sells nothing and
buys nothing: its button returns to the URL field.

Mercado Pago takes the money today, through a Payment Brick embedded in the page: Pix, card and
boleto, and a CPF is enough to receive. Stripe stays wired behind the same buttons for when there is
a company to register it to.

The reasoning worth keeping:

- **Credits are not a subscription.** A page owner audits twice a year, and a monthly plan *on that
  usage* is a cancellation waiting to happen.

  **A monitoring subscription is the shape to avoid here.** Billing monthly to re-measure the page
  and mail the owner what moved delivers silence most months: the mail fires only on a regression,
  deliberately (see [invariants.md](invariants.md)), and a landing page nobody has touched does not
  regress. The subscriber pays monthly to be told nothing, which is not a perception problem to be
  fixed with copy. It is also dominated by the packs next to it, since a monthly fee beside a pack
  card asks the reader to pay more per analysis for the privilege of being watched.

  Paid acquisition arithmetic is answered by the price of the analysis instead: the purchases have
  to repay the clicks that produced them. See [ads.md](ads.md).
- **The pack sizes are a question as much as an offer.** Someone with one landing page needs one;
  someone with a funnel needs three. There is no pack of ten: at R$9,90 an analysis it would make
  the cheapest thing on the page the one this reader has no use for.

  **This is also why the trio's discount is per analysis and never in credits.** Handing over five
  for the price of three would cost a few reais of tokens and buy the reader nothing, because they
  own one page or three. More credits is not more value here; it is the pack of ten again, wearing
  a discount.
- **The price is set against the click, and it was not always.** An analysis costs a couple of reais
  to produce and sells for R$97, so the margin was never the constraint. It sold for R$47 until the
  funnel arithmetic in [ads.md](ads.md) was worked through against the funnel that actually exists:
  at R$47 a cohort of paid clicks lost money in every scenario, including the optimistic one, so the
  campaign could never stay on long enough for the mailing list to start compounding.

  R$19 and R$39 were earlier still, and failed the same test harder. The direction of the error was
  always the same one.

  **Whether the Brazilian buyer pays R$97 is a bet, and it is written down as one.** R$97 is an
  ordinary price point there and the deliverable is freelancer-shaped; against that, the reader has
  never heard of us and the free half already hands them the diagnosis. Nobody measured either side
  and, at this volume, nobody will -- see the price-test argument in [ads.md](ads.md). What is
  settled is that R$47 was not the safer number.
- **Granting is provider-agnostic**, keyed on `(provider, provider_ref)`. Stripe cannot charge in BRL
  without a registered company, which is exactly why the second provider exists, and the adapter
  shape is what kept adding it from being a rewrite: `lib/credits.ts` did not change. See
  [invariants.md](invariants.md#credits-are-granted-by-one-internal-path-and-no-provider-code-touches-the-tables).
- **A row may exist before its first sign-in.** Someone can pay before they have ever opened the app;
  the webhook creates the row holding the credits, and the first sign-in claims it.

## The funnel does not end at the first visit

A funnel that ends there is the hole under the whole advertising plan: someone pastes a URL, reads a
free score, does not buy, and there is no path back. It matters most where it is least visible, in
the arithmetic. The break-even table in [ads.md](ads.md) divides the cost of a click by the
click-to-purchase rate, and with no return path that rate can only ever be the first visit's.

Three things keep it open, and all three are first-party:

- **The sequence.** Day 2 carries one thing counted on the reader's own page; day 7 says what is
  behind the unlock. Then it stops.
- **The pending payment reminder.** Pix and boleto are chosen because they settle after the browser
  has gone, so an unfinished checkout is ordinary and something has to follow one.
- **Customer Match.** The addresses become an ads audience, hashed. See [ads.md](ads.md) for what it
  cost in the privacy policy.

**What it does not change is the price.** A funnel makes a lower ticket *possible* by raising the
rate the break-even divides by; it does not make one necessary, and the choice between a lower price
and a wider margin is not worth making before there is a measured rate to make it against.

## What it deliberately does not do

- **No white-label, no plans, no PDF.** All three belong to a reader who sells audits to other
  people. **Lead capture is not on this list**: what the rule forbids is an email *wall* trading a
  stranger's address for a preview of someone else's report. What stands asks below the numbers,
  gates nothing, and gives the reader the one durable copy of a link that otherwise lives in a single
  browser
- **Sign-in is OAuth only**, so no password is ever stored and nothing is mailed to authenticate
  anybody. Mail that does go out is the report link, a two-mail follow-up, and a reminder about a
  payment the provider still reports as pending. **Every one of them is leaveable in one click**, and
  none of them is required to use anything
- **No live A/B testing, no snippet, no significance.** See the section above for why
- **No third-party tracker on any surface.** This is not "no tracking" flatly: the product buys
  traffic, so a paid click leaves a first-party cookie holding its Google Ads click id, and a
  confirmed payment is reported to Google from the server. What holds, and is the part worth stating,
  is that **nothing is loaded from a third party into any page** -- no tag manager, no analytics script, no pixel. The distinction is not
  pedantry: the product charges people to be told their page is heavy, so a tag container on our own
  landing page would be it failing its own audit. See [ads.md](ads.md)
- **No causal claim on any surface.** Nothing states what a change will produce, because nothing here
  controlled for anything, see
  [invariants.md](invariants.md#a-delta-is-arithmetic-between-two-measurements-never-a-result-attributed-to-a-change)
- **No competitor is ever inferred, and none is ever searched for.** The product can compare against a
  second page, and only against one the reader typed into the form: it is then scraped and counted by
  the same code as the first. Having a model recall what competitors do is the thing this refuses,
  see
  [invariants.md](invariants.md#a-generated-evidence-carries-a-number-only-from-a-page-this-code-measured)
